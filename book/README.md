## What it is

*The $100 Question: We Tested Every Trading Strategy We Could Find* is a complete audit of what actually makes money in the stock market, run as an experiment instead of an argument. Every system the project could build and test honestly was built and tested: value, momentum, quality, trend following, mean reversion, dividend rules, machine learning pickers and vetoes, and dozens more, including the ones that failed, and the failures are printed at the same length as the wins. Each strategy ran on the same yardstick, a $100 pot, under the same rules frozen in advance, with costs charged, against the same opponent: SPY, the index fund anyone can buy.

The question in the title is the method. If a system cannot beat the index on a hundred dollars, under rules written down before the test, with its worst years printed beside its best, it does not get to call itself a way to make money.

## The headline record, exactly as the book states it

One system came through the whole audit: the champion, value plus momentum, five equal positions picked quarterly from a pond of 30 large companies as they stood in November 2016, under a plain 15 percent floor. Its record, $100 start, the book's cost convention, historical replay:

* 2019 to 2026: the champion turned $100 into **$544.47**. SPY turned the same $100 into $313.62.
* The judging years, 2023 to 2026: the champion finished at **$288.05** against SPY at $187.77.
* The decade window: $667.35.
* 2010 to 2026: **$1,415.62** against SPY at $822.94.

And the other half of the record, which the book prints just as large:

* The worst peak to trough slide was about **36 percent**. Anyone running this system would have lived through that.
* In March 2020 the pot's closest approach to its floor left **$8.13** of room. The floor was nearly hit, and the book says so.
* Scored year by year in folds, the champion won 4 and **lost 3**. It loses years, in print, by name.
* The book's own estimate, after its planning haircut, is 18 to 23 percent a year if the edge holds at all, against 24 to 31 percent in replay, and its stated belief that the edge is real is 65 to 70 in 100. Not a certainty. A stated, numbered doubt.

These are historical replay results.
